# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. Assume the person on the other side is trying to help,
and say what you mean without making it personal.

## What is expected

- Criticise code, decisions and ideas as sharply as you like. Do not criticise
  people.
- Take disagreement as information, not as an attack. „This breaks X" is useful;
  answer the X.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a contribution. A project has a direction,
  and not every good change fits it.
- Assume good faith in a report or a question, including a badly worded one. Not
  everyone writes English comfortably.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments; personal or political attacks;
publishing someone's private information; sexualised language or imagery; sustained
disruption of discussions. Anything that makes a reasonable person not want to
participate.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, in pull requests and commit messages, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately through a
[security advisory](https://github.com/dermellor/zeitlines/security/advisories/new)
on this repository — it is the one channel here that is not world-readable, so it
works for conduct reports as well as vulnerabilities. Do not open a public issue,
because the tracker is public and that would expose the very details a report needs
to keep private.

Reports go to the maintainers, who will respond as fast as they reasonably can.
This is a small project, not a company with a process behind it: expect a human
reply, not a ticket number. A report will be kept confidential to the people
handling it.

## Consequences

Maintainers may edit or delete comments, close issues and pull requests, or block
an account from the repository. Which of those applies is a judgement call about
keeping the project workable, not a tariff.
